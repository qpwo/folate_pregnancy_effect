The three creatine reports above are randomized ANIMAL experiments.
Tran administered creatine directly to fetal sheep, not maternal oral dosing.
Czeizel 1993 reports the Hungarian trial; it does not replace the 1992 NEJM
original when tracking which publications have been obtained.

No human pregnancy creatine supplementation RCT was verified in this search.
Naidu 2025 (PMC12291183) is an open-label pharmacokinetic conference abstract.
The 2014 Cochrane review found no human RCTs through November 2014 only.
The current search is not proof that no later trial exists.

Corrections to earlier candidate descriptions:
- Peters' folic-acid/creatine arsenic trial is not a pregnancy trial.
- EAGeR randomizes aspirin, not folate.
- Berry 1999 is not a randomized supplementation trial.
- JMEST neonatal-jaundice paper is not verified as a randomized experiment.
- PMC11032415 is observational, despite random sampling terminology.
- Protocols, reviews and conference abstracts are not full RCT reports.
- Follow-up publications must not be counted as independent experiments.
- Trials varying several nutrients cannot isolate folate effects.
